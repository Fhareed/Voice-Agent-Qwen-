# LumiDesk — Customer Interview Field Guide
### Beachhead: plumbers / LVI (trades) in Oulu

This is a **problem interview, not a sales call.** Your only job is to find out whether
missed phone calls are a *real, frequent, and expensive* problem for Oulu plumbers —
**before** you build or sell anything. If you pitch LumiDesk, they'll be polite, tell you
"nice idea," and you'll learn nothing true.

---

## The 4 golden rules (read before every call)

1. **Talk about their life and business, not your idea.** Don't mention LumiDesk until the very end.
2. **Ask about the past, not the future.** "What did you do last time…" beats "Would you ever…". People are honest about what they *did* and unreliable about what they *would* do.
3. **Shut up and listen.** Aim for them talking 80% of the time. Silence is your friend — let them fill it.
4. **Don't sell, don't defend, don't correct.** If they say something that kills your idea, that's the most valuable answer you can get. Write it down and thank them.

---

## Logistics

- **How many:** Do **5–10** interviews before you decide anything. Patterns only appear after ~5.
- **How long:** 15–20 minutes each. Coffee, phone, or standing in their shop.
- **Where to find them:**
  - Google Maps → search `putkiliike Oulu`, `LVI Oulu`, `putkimies Oulu`, `viemärinavaus Oulu`
  - Fonecta / Suomen Yritysrekisteri, local trade Facebook groups
  - Just call the numbers — a plumber picking up *is itself* your first data point; one who doesn't is your second.
- **Recording:** Ask first ("Voinko nauhoittaa, vain omiin muistiinpanoihini?"). If no, write notes the instant you hang up.
- **Bring nothing to sell.** No slides, no demo. A notebook only.

# The script

Each question is given in **English** (so you know the intent) and **Finnish** (what you actually say).
The Finnish is standard *yleiskieli* — a native Oulu speaker can make it warmer; feel free to soften it.

## 0 · Open — set a no-pressure frame

> **EN:** Hi, thanks for taking a few minutes. I'm not selling anything — I'm a founder here in Oulu trying to understand how small trade businesses handle their phone calls and customers. Can I ask you a few questions about how your business actually works day to day? There are no right answers.

> **FI:** Moi, kiitos että ehdit hetken. En ole myymässä mitään — olen yrittäjä täältä Oulusta ja yritän ymmärtää, miten pienet LVI- ja putkialan yritykset hoitavat puheluita ja asiakkaita. Voinko kysyä muutaman kysymyksen siitä, miten yrityksesi arjessa oikeasti toimii? Oikeita vastauksia ei ole.

## 1 · Their business — how work comes in

> **EN:** How long have you been running the business, and how many of you are there?
> **FI:** Kuinka kauan olet pyörittänyt yritystä, ja kuinka monta teitä on?

> **EN:** When a new customer needs you, how do they usually get in touch — phone, WhatsApp, referrals, your website?
> **FI:** Kun uusi asiakas tarvitsee sinua, miten hän yleensä ottaa yhteyttä — puhelin, WhatsApp, suositukset, nettisivut?

> **EN:** Roughly how many calls do you get on a normal day?
> **FI:** Suunnilleen kuinka monta puhelua saat tavallisena päivänä?

## 2 · The calls — what actually happens (the core; go slow here)

> **EN:** Think about the last time your phone rang while you were under a sink or in the middle of a job. Walk me through exactly what you did.
> **FI:** Mieti viime kertaa, kun puhelin soi kesken työn, vaikka lavuaarin alla. Kerro tarkalleen, mitä teit.

> **EN:** When you genuinely can't pick up, what happens to that call?
> **FI:** Kun et oikeasti pysty vastaamaan, mitä sille puhelulle tapahtuu?

> **EN:** When did you last notice a missed call? What was it, did you call back, and did you get the job?
> **FI:** Milloin viimeksi huomasit vastaamattoman puhelun? Mikä se oli, soititko takaisin, ja saitko sen työn?

## 3 · Quantify the pain (get numbers, not adjectives)

> **EN:** In a normal week, how many calls do you reckon you miss?
> **FI:** Tavallisella viikolla, kuinka monta puhelua arvioit jääväsi vastaamatta?

> **EN:** When a new customer can't reach you, what do you think they do next?
> **FI:** Kun uusi asiakas ei tavoita sinua, mitä luulet hänen tekevän seuraavaksi?

> **EN:** What's a typical job worth to you, roughly?
> **FI:** Paljonko tavallinen keikka on sinulle suunnilleen arvoltaan?

> **EN:** Have you ever found out you lost a job specifically because you didn't pick up?
> **FI:** Oletko koskaan saanut tietää menettäneesi työn juuri siksi, ettet vastannut?

## 4 · What they've already tried — this is your real competition

> **EN:** Have you ever tried anything to deal with this — an answering service, a secretary, a family member answering, a second phone, voicemail-to-text?
> **FI:** Oletko koskaan kokeillut mitään tähän — puhelinvastauspalvelua, sihteeriä, perheenjäsentä vastaajana, toista puhelinta, vastaajaviestien tekstitystä?

*If yes:*
> **EN:** What made you start? What made you stop, or what didn't you like? And what did it cost?
> **FI:** Mikä sai sinut aloittamaan? Mikä sai lopettamaan, tai mikä siinä ei toiminut? Ja paljonko se maksoi?

*If no:*
> **EN:** Have you ever thought about it? What's held you back?
> **FI:** Oletko koskaan miettinyt sitä? Mikä on pidätellyt?

## 5 · After-hours & emergencies (plumber-specific gold)

> **EN:** Do you get calls in the evenings or weekends? Real emergencies, like a burst pipe?
> **FI:** Tuleeko sinulle puheluita iltaisin tai viikonloppuisin? Oikeita hätätapauksia, kuten putkirikko?

> **EN:** What happens to those calls right now?
> **FI:** Mitä niille puheluille tapahtuu tällä hetkellä?

## 6 · The AI-trust probe (soft — just listen, capture their exact words)

> **EN:** Some businesses are starting to use an automated or AI assistant to answer the phone when they can't. Setting my own opinion aside — how do you feel about that idea for a business like yours?
> **FI:** Jotkut yritykset ovat alkaneet käyttää automaattista tai tekoälyavustajaa vastaamaan puhelimeen silloin, kun he eivät itse pysty. Oma mielipiteeni sivuun — miltä sellainen ajatus tuntuisi sinun kaltaisellesi yritykselle?

> **EN:** What would worry you about a computer talking to your customers?
> **FI:** Mikä sinua huolestuttaisi siinä, että tietokone puhuu asiakkaidesi kanssa?

*(Write their words down verbatim. This is the real test of your "Finns are skeptical of AI" thesis — you need to hear whether it's a dealbreaker or just a first reaction.)*

## 7 · Willingness to pay (anchored — never ask a hypothetical price)

Do **not** ask "would you pay €250/month?". Instead, uncover what they already spend:

> **EN:** What do you spend today on getting and handling customer calls — your phone plan, any service, and your own time?
> **FI:** Paljonko käytät nykyään asiakaspuheluiden hoitamiseen — puhelinliittymä, mahdollinen palvelu, ja oma aikasi?

> **EN:** If you hired someone part-time just to answer the phone, what would that roughly cost you?
> **FI:** Jos palkkaisit jonkun osa-aikaisesti pelkästään vastaamaan puhelimeen, paljonko se suunnilleen maksaisi?

*Only if it comes up naturally:*
> **EN:** If something reliably caught the calls you miss and booked them for you, what would that be worth to you a month?
> **FI:** Jos jokin luotettavasti nappaisi ne puhelut, jotka jäävät sinulta, ja varaisi ne puolestasi, paljonko se olisi sinulle arvoltaan kuussa?

**Let them say the number.** The number they name tells you more than any figure you propose.

## 8 · Close & referral

> **EN:** This was really helpful, thank you. Can I come back to you if I have another question?
> **FI:** Tämä oli tosi hyödyllistä, kiitos. Voinko palata asiaan, jos minulla on lisää kysyttävää?

> **EN:** Do you know one or two other plumbers or tradespeople I should talk to?
> **FI:** Tunnetko pari muuta putkimiestä tai muuta yrittäjää, joiden kanssa minun kannattaisi jutella?

*Only now, and only if you want them as a first customer:*
> **EN:** I'm actually building something in this exact area. Would you be open to seeing it when it's ready?
> **FI:** Rakennan itse asiassa jotain juuri tähän aiheeseen. Olisitko avoin näkemään sen, kun se on valmis?

# After each interview — fill this in (2 minutes, immediately)

| Signal | What to capture |
|---|---|
| **Do they miss calls?** | How many per week? |
| **Is it expensive?** | € per job × missed calls = € bleeding per week |
| **Have they PAID to fix it before?** | ← the single strongest buying signal |
| **Their exact words on AI** | quote them, don't paraphrase |
| **Did they name a price / ask to see it?** | ← this person is a warm lead |
| **Referral given?** | name + number |

---

# Your decision rule (after 5–10 interviews)

- **Problem is weak** → if most say missed calls are rare, cheap, or "I always call back and it's fine." → *rethink the segment or the problem before building more.*
- **Problem is strong** → if most miss valuable calls, have already tried or paid for a fix, and a few asked to see it. → *those few people are your first customers. Go build for them specifically.*

The goal of these calls is **not** to confirm you're right. It's to find out fast if you're
wrong — while it's still cheap to change course. That's the whole point of the 100 days.
